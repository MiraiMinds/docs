## One stack on every tier

Every tier runs on the same Mira stack. **The price is the only difference.**
Choose a tier for what you want to commit to, not for what the calls can do.

**Single-prompt and node-based agents are available on every tier.** Two
limits apply today:

- **Node-based agents run as browser (web) calls.** Phone calls for flows are
  coming. Until then, a phone call or campaign with a node-based agent is
  refused with `409 flow_agent_web_only` — nothing is dialled and nothing is
  charged.
- **Creating node-based agents in Agent Studio is rolling out.**

## Your plan

A company is on one plan, and every call it makes is billed at that plan's
rate.

- **Start today on T5.** Complete **Go live** in the
  [console](https://sandbox.voice.miraiminds.co) — it is self-serve, with no
  monthly commitment. You pay for the minutes you use from prepaid credit.
- **Move up by committing.** ₹50,000 a month puts the company on T3;
  ₹1,00,000 a month puts it on T1.
- **Commitments become credit.** Each monthly commitment is credited to your
  wallet, and unused credit never expires.
- **Top up at the same rate.** Credit you add is spent at your plan's rate.

Prices exclude GST. Calls are billed in 10-second blocks.

## What a minute costs

Billing is **pro-rated in 10-second blocks, rounded up, with a one-block
minimum.** Rates are quoted per minute; the billing unit is a sixth of one. A
part-block is a whole block, and nothing smaller than 10 seconds is billed.

### Metering

`duration_secs` on the [call object](/v2/calls#the-call-object) is the true
media duration in seconds. The charge is that duration rounded **up** to the
next 10 seconds — never less than one block — times your plan's per-minute
rate, divided by 60 and rounded **up** to the paisa. On T1 the per-minute rate
is the ₹1.50 end-to-end rate, because telco and insights are charged with the
call. A call that never connected is not billed at all — see the table below.

```python
def cost_paise(duration_secs: int, rate_paise_per_min: int) -> int:
    billed_secs = max(1, -(-duration_secs // 10)) * 10  # whole 10-second blocks
    return -(-billed_secs * rate_paise_per_min // 60)    # rounded up to the paisa

cost_paise(96, 300)  # 100 billed seconds on T3 -> 500 paise, ₹5.00
```

You never have to compute this — `cost_inr` on the call object and the
debit row in the [ledger](/v2/wallet#list-transactions) are authoritative. Use
the formula to forecast, not to reconcile.

### What is and is not billable

| Outcome | `status` | Billed |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Conversation completed | `completed` | ✅ |
| Answering machine, message left or hung up on | `voicemail` | ✅ |
| Hit the duration cap | `timeout` | ✅ |
| Rang out | `no_answer` | ❌ |
| Line busy | `busy` | ❌ |
| Could not connect, or our pipeline errored | `failed` | ❌ |
| You cancelled before it connected | `aborted` | ❌ |

The rule underneath: **if media went live, you pay for it.** A voicemail
consumed a real dial, real TTS and real carrier time, so it is billed. A call
our own pipeline broke is not.

### Forecasting a month

Take your answer rate and your mean answered-call duration. Round each answered
call up to a whole 10 seconds first, then multiply. The rounding is small, but
it never cancels out, so averaging the seconds first will under-forecast
slightly.

```text
monthly ₹ = answered_calls × mean(ceil(secs / 10)) × rate / 6
```

A campaign of 50,000 dials at a 35% answer rate and 80 seconds mean talk time:

{/* pricing:forecast:start */}

```text
50,000 × 0.35 = 17,500 answered
80 seconds -> 8 billed blocks each
T5 agent usage: ₹1,16,666.67
T3 agent usage: ₹70,000
T1 agent usage: ₹23,333.33
```

These are usage charges. T3 and T1 monthly commitments still apply. T5 has no monthly commitment. Unused wallet credit carries forward.

{/* pricing:forecast:end */}

Unanswered dials cost nothing, so the 32,500 that rang out are free.

With a 10-second block **the cost tracks talk time almost linearly** — there is
no minute boundary to engineer around. Every block you cut off the mean reduces
the cost of every answered call. A prompt that caps reply length is still the
cheapest way to move it, and a call that ends the moment its job is done pays
for nothing after that.

## Wallet

Billing is prepaid against one INR wallet per workspace.

```bash
curl https://sandbox.voice.miraiminds.co/v2/wallet \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_live_YOUR_API_KEY"
```

```json
{ "balance_inr": 500, "currency": "INR", "updated_at": "2026-07-26T09:12:44Z" }
```

- **Debits happen at call end**, one ledger row per billable call, carrying the
  `call_id`.
- **Top up from the console.** Once your company has gone live, add credit
  from the **Billing** page of the
  [console](https://sandbox.voice.miraiminds.co) whenever you need it. On T3
  and T1, each monthly commitment is credited to the same wallet. Before you
  go live, your Mirai contact credits the workspace.
- **Credit never expires.** Unused credit carries forward from month to month.
- **The ledger is the source of truth** for reconciliation:
  [`GET /v2/wallet/transactions`](/v2/wallet#list-transactions).

See the [Wallet reference](/v2/wallet) for the full API.

## At zero balance

**Before dialling**, we check the wallet can cover at least one minute at the
call's tier. Billing is per 10-second block, but the pre-dial check asks for a
full minute of headroom. If the wallet cannot cover it:

```json title="402 Payment Required"
{
  "error": {
    "code": "insufficient_balance",
    "message": "wallet balance 0.40 INR is below the minimum for one minute at t5"
  }
}
```

- No phone rings. No charge. No call object is created.
- The `Idempotency-Key` is **not** consumed — retry with the same key after
  topping up.
- **Calls already in flight are not killed.** A call that started with credit
  runs to its natural end and then debits. Your balance can therefore dip below
  what the pre-dial check implied during a busy minute.

- **Inside a [campaign](/v2/campaigns) this is not an error.** The campaign
  pauses itself with `pause_reason: "insufficient_balance"`, keeps every
  contact's place, and continues from there once you top up and start it again.

Alert on balance yourself, hourly, at a threshold covering a day of traffic. A
`402` mid-campaign is an expensive way to find out.

## Setting the tier

Your plan sets the tier. A key created after your company goes live carries
your plan's tier, and every call and every campaign placed with it inherits
that tier — leave `tier` out of the request:

```json
{
  "agent_id": "agt_01K7Q9F3K7M2N5P9R4T6V8W0XZ",
  "to": "+919876543210"
}
```

If you do send `tier`, it must be your plan's. A call at any other tier is
refused with `403 contract_unavailable` before anything is dialled. There is no
per-call switch to a different rate: to change what your calls cost, change
your plan.

The call object reports the `tier` it ran at alongside `cost_inr`, so you can
always see which rate card a given call was billed under. A
[campaign](/v2/campaigns) takes its tier at create time, and every call it
places inherits it.
