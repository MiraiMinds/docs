---
title: "Bring your own SIP trunk"
description: "Point your carrier's SIP trunk at Mirai. Your agents answer its numbers and call from them, and your carrier keeps billing you for the line."
---

If you already have a SIP trunk with Indian numbers on it, you can connect it to
Mirai. Your agents answer calls to the trunk's numbers and place calls from
them. Your carrier keeps billing you for the numbers and the minutes, and we
bill only for your agents' minutes.

Once it's added, a number on your trunk works like any other number here. An
agent answers it, it can sit in a [pool](/v2/phone-numbers), and you can pick it
under **Calls come from** for a campaign or an agent.

## What you need

- A SIP trunk from a carrier, with one or more Indian numbers on it.
- These details from your carrier: their SIP address and port, UDP or TCP, the
  IP addresses they send calls from, and how they write phone numbers. Some
  carriers also give you a dial prefix, or a username and password.
- A published [agent](/v2/agents) to answer the calls.

### What a trunk can do today

| | Supported |
| :--- | :--- |
| Signalling | SIP over UDP or TCP, port 5060 |
| Audio | Plain RTP with G.711 μ-law (PCMU) or A-law (PCMA) |
| How your carrier knows us | By our IP address. On calls we send, also by a username and password if your carrier asks for one. |
| Numbers | Indian numbers (+91 and 10 digits), and calls to Indian numbers |

Not supported yet: encrypted calls (TLS and SRTP), registering with your
carrier (SIP REGISTER), keypad tones (DTMF relay), call transfer, and numbers
outside India. If your carrier insists on one of these, the trunk won't work
with Mirai yet.

## Step 1: Connect the trunk

1. Open **Phone numbers** in the [console](https://console.voice.miraiminds.co)
   and click **Get a number**.
2. Choose **Connect your SIP trunk**.
3. Pick your carrier: **Plivo**, **VoiceLink** or **Other**. A preset fills in
   what it can. Check every value against what your carrier gave you.
4. Fill in the rest of the form and click **Connect SIP trunk**.

| Field | What to enter |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Connection name** | A name you'll recognise, such as "Office SIP trunk". |
| **Carrier's SIP address** and **Port** | Where your carrier takes calls: a host name like `sip.carrier.example` or a public IP address. The port is usually 5060. Put the port in **Port**, not in the address. |
| **Transport** | UDP or TCP, whichever your carrier uses. |
| **How your carrier writes numbers** | `+919876543210` (+91 first), `919876543210` (91 first), `09876543210` (0 first) or `9876543210` (10 digits only). We write numbers this way on the calls we send to your carrier. |
| **Dial prefix** (optional) | Only if your carrier gave you a tech prefix. Digits only, up to 12. We put it in front of every number we dial, so `45454` with 91 first dials `45454919876543210`. |
| **Username** and **Password** (optional) | Only if your carrier wants our calls to sign in (digest authentication). The password is encrypted and never shown again. |
| **Your carrier's IP addresses** | The addresses your carrier sends calls from, one per line, up to 16. A range like `198.51.100.0/28` works too, up to /24. We answer calls only from these addresses. A call from anywhere else gets no reply at all. |

You can change any of this later with **Edit** on the trunk's card. Leave the
password blank to keep the saved one. Changes apply from the next call.

## Step 2: Give your carrier our details

After you connect, the trunk's card opens at **Point your trunk at us**. You'll
find the card later under **Phone numbers → Accounts → Your SIP trunks**. Each
value has a **Copy** button, and **Copy all** copies them together, ready to
paste into an email to your carrier.

The card is the source of truth. If it ever differs from this page, use the
card. Today it shows:

| On the card | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| SIP address | `sip.miraiminds.co:5060` |
| Transport | UDP or TCP |
| Signalling IP | `168.220.248.249` |
| Media IP | `168.220.248.249` |
| Media ports | UDP `20000-29999` |
| Codecs | PCMU, PCMA |

Ask your carrier to:

- Send calls for your numbers to `sip.miraiminds.co`, port 5060, over the
  transport you chose.
- Accept calls and audio from `168.220.248.249`.
- Send and receive audio on UDP ports 20000 to 29999 at that address, as PCMU
  or PCMA without encryption.

If you run a firewall or a session border controller between your carrier and
us, open the same address and ports there.

## Step 3: Add your numbers

A SIP trunk has no number list for us to read, so you type each number in.

1. On the trunk's card, click **Add a number**.
2. Enter the number: an Indian number, +91 and 10 digits, such as
   `+91 98765 43210`.
3. Under **Who answers**, choose the agent that answers calls to it.
4. Click **Add number**.

The number is ready straight away. There's nothing to turn on, so calls reach
the agent as soon as your carrier sends them to us. A number can belong to only
one Mirai workspace.

On incoming calls, your carrier can write the number you were called on in any
of the four formats above. We read them all.

## Step 4: Test both directions

1. Call one of the numbers from another phone. You should hear your agent's
   greeting.
2. Call out from it. Click **Call my phone** on the trunk's card, pick the
   number under **Calls come from** in a small test campaign, or place an API
   call with its `phone_number_id`.
3. On each call, check that both sides hear each other. Hang up once from each
   end.
4. Look at the trunk's card. It shows the last call in and the last call out,
   with the reason if one failed. It also lists calls we turned away because
   they came from an address that isn't on your list.

Test before you print the number anywhere or start a real campaign on it.

## Billing

Calls on your own trunk carry no Mirai line charge. Your carrier bills you for
the numbers and the minutes. Your agents' minutes are billed at your plan's
rate, the same as any other call. See [Billing & tiers](/general/tiers).

## Carrier walkthroughs

### Plivo

Plivo calls its SIP trunking Zentrunk. In the Plivo console, open **SIP
Trunking** and:

1. Create an IP access list with our IP address, `168.220.248.249`.
2. Create an outbound trunk that uses that list. Plivo gives the trunk a domain
   that looks like `123456789.zt.plivo.com`. You'll enter it in Mirai.
3. Create an origination URI, `sip.miraiminds.co:5060`, and an inbound trunk
   that uses it.
4. Attach your Plivo number to the inbound trunk.

<Warning>
Your Plivo account must be in Plivo's India region to use Indian numbers.
</Warning>

Then connect the trunk in Mirai and choose **Plivo**:

| Field | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Carrier's SIP address** | Your outbound trunk's domain, such as `123456789.zt.plivo.com` |
| **Port** | `5060` |
| **Transport** | UDP |
| **How your carrier writes numbers** | `+919876543210` (+91 first) |
| **Your carrier's IP addresses** | Plivo's published ranges, which the preset fills in: `13.52.9.0/25`, `18.214.109.128/25`, `18.215.142.0/26`, `204.89.148.128/26`, `216.120.187.128/26` |

Then add the number you attached to the inbound trunk, as in
[Step 3](#step-3-add-your-numbers).

Mirai can also use a Plivo number without a trunk: connect your Plivo account
with its Auth ID and Auth Token instead. See
[Receive inbound phone calls](/cookbooks/inbound-calling).

### VoiceLink

In the VoiceLink panel:

1. Open **Voice Services → SIP Trunk Management** and click **Add SIP Trunk**.
2. Set **BOT provider** to **Custom**, and enter our SIP address and IP address
   from [Step 2](#step-2-give-your-carrier-our-details).
3. Tick the numbers (DIDs) that should come to Mirai.
4. Note the **Tech Prefix** the panel shows for your trunk.

Then connect the trunk in Mirai and choose **VoiceLink**:

| Field | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Carrier's SIP address** | `sip.voicelink.co.in` |
| **Port** | `3300` |
| **Transport** | UDP |
| **How your carrier writes numbers** | Check with VoiceLink. Start with `9876543210` (10 digits only). |
| **Dial prefix** | The Tech Prefix from your VoiceLink panel |
| **Your carrier's IP addresses** | `160.30.71.108`, `160.30.71.111`, `160.30.71.112`, `160.30.71.113` |

### Other carriers

Choose **Other** and ask your carrier for:

- The SIP address and port to send calls to, and UDP or TCP.
- Every IP address they send calls from.
- How they want numbers written, and their tech prefix if they use one.
- A username and password, if they want our calls to sign in.

Send them the values from **Point your trunk at us**. Check that they can use
G.711 (PCMU or PCMA) without encryption, and that they don't need us to
register with them.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| Calls to your number ring out or never reach the agent | Open the trunk's card. If it lists calls turned away from an address that isn't on your list, click **Add this address**, but only if your carrier confirms the address is theirs. Also check that the number is added to this trunk and has an agent to answer it. |
| A call out fails as refused (403) | Your carrier turned the call down. Ask them to allow `168.220.248.249` and the number you call from (your caller ID). If your carrier gave you a username and password, check them with **Edit**. |
| A call out fails as not found (404) | Your carrier didn't recognise the number the way we wrote it. Under **Edit**, change **How your carrier writes numbers** or the **Dial prefix**, then call again. |
| One-way audio, or no audio | Open UDP ports 20000 to 29999 to and from `168.220.248.249` on your side. Make sure your carrier doesn't require encrypted audio (SRTP). |
| Busy or no answer | These are normal call results: the person was busy or didn't pick up. |

## Set it up through the API

The API takes the same steps. Use your workspace's
[API host](/cookbooks/inbound-calling#1-choose-your-api-host) and key, and the
id of the published agent that should answer:

```bash
set -euo pipefail
: "${VOICE_BASE_URL:?Set your assigned API host}"
: "${VOICE_API_KEY:?Set your workspace API key}"
: "${AGENT_ID:?Set the id of a published agent}"
```

### 1. Read what to give your carrier

`GET /v2/telephony/options` carries `sip` only when SIP trunks are on for your
workspace. These are the values the console shows under **Point your trunk at
us**.

```bash
curl --fail-with-body -sS "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/telephony/options" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY" | jq '.sip'
```

```json
{
  "address": "sip.miraiminds.co",
  "port": 5060,
  "transports": ["udp", "tcp"],
  "signalling_ips": ["168.220.248.249"],
  "media_ips": ["168.220.248.249"],
  "media_ports": "20000-29999",
  "codecs": ["PCMU", "PCMA"],
  "number_formats": ["e164", "e164_digits", "national", "local"]
}
```

### 2. Connect the trunk

```bash
TRUNK=$(curl --fail-with-body -sS "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/telephony/connections" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{
    "provider": "sip",
    "name": "Office SIP trunk",
    "sip": {
      "host": "sip.carrier.example",
      "port": 5060,
      "transport": "udp",
      "number_format": "e164",
      "inbound_sources": ["203.0.113.10", "198.51.100.0/28"]
    }
  }')
CONNECTION_ID=$(jq -er '.id' <<< "$TRUNK")
```

| `sip` field | Required | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `host` | yes | Your carrier's SIP address: a host name or a public IPv4 address. |
| `port` | no | 1 to 65535. Default 5060. |
| `transport` | no | `udp` or `tcp`. |
| `inbound_sources` | yes | 1 to 16 public IPv4 addresses or ranges your carrier calls from, each /24 or narrower. A bare address means /32. |
| `number_format` | no | `e164` (+919876543210), `e164_digits` (919876543210), `national` (09876543210) or `local` (9876543210). |
| `dial_prefix` | no | Your carrier's tech prefix. Digits only, up to 12. `""` means none. |
| `username`, `password` | no | Only if your carrier wants our calls to sign in. The password never comes back. |

The answer is `201` with the trunk. Its `state` is `verified` at once, because
nothing is checked with your carrier. Its `sip` holds the settings, with
`has_password` in place of the password.

### 3. Add a number and choose who answers

The number is `ready` as soon as it's added. Assign an agent with the number's
current `version`.

```bash
NUMBER=$(jq -n --arg c "$CONNECTION_ID" '{connection_id: $c, number: "+918045671234"}' |
  curl --fail-with-body -sS "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/phone-numbers" \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY" \
    -H 'Content-Type: application/json' --data-binary @-)
NUMBER_ID=$(jq -er '.id' <<< "$NUMBER")

jq --arg agent "$AGENT_ID" '{version, agent_id: $agent}' <<< "$NUMBER" |
  curl --fail-with-body -sS -X PATCH "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/phone-numbers/$NUMBER_ID" \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY" \
    -H 'Content-Type: application/json' --data-binary @-
```

To place a call from the number, send its id as `phone_number_id` on
[`POST /v2/calls`](/v2/calls#create-a-call), or add it to a
[pool](/v2/phone-numbers#create-a-pool).

### 4. Change the trunk later

`PATCH /v2/telephony/connections/{id}` takes the trunk's current `version` and,
in `sip`, only the fields that change. Leave `password` out to keep it, or send
`null` to remove it. Send `"dial_prefix": ""` to remove the prefix.
`"state": "disabled"` turns the trunk off. Changes apply from the next call.

```bash
TRUNK=$(curl --fail-with-body -sS "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/telephony/connections/$CONNECTION_ID" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY")

jq '{version, sip: {inbound_sources: ["203.0.113.10", "203.0.113.11"]}}' <<< "$TRUNK" |
  curl --fail-with-body -sS -X PATCH "$VOICE_BASE_URL/v2/telephony/connections/$CONNECTION_ID" \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $VOICE_API_KEY" \
    -H 'Content-Type: application/json' --data-binary @-
```

Every request, with example answers, is in the
[Postman collection](/api-reference/postman).

### Errors

| Status | `error.code` | When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `400` | `provider_unavailable` | SIP trunks aren't on for your workspace. |
| `400` | `invalid_request` | A field is out of range: a private or local carrier address, a range wider than /24, more than 16 addresses, or a number that isn't +91 and 10 digits. `error.message` names the field. |
| `400` | `not_supported` | `GET /v2/telephony/connections/{id}/numbers` on a trunk. A trunk has no number list, so add its numbers by number. |
| `409` | `conflict` | The number is already on Mirai, or the `version` you sent is no longer current. |
